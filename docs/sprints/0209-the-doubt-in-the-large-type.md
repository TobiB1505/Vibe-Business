# Sprint 0209 — The doubt in the large type

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Decision:** module nine is the objections, and it is the one block on the page whose loudest text belongs to the reader.

## The gap it closes

Named as missing since the walk started. Eight blocks explain what Vibe does and one asks for money; nothing anywhere met the sentence a founder is actually saying while they read, which for this product is some version of *"I am not letting an AI near the repository I ship from."*

## The ninth shape is an inversion

Every other block puts Vibe's claim in the large type and the qualification in the small type. This one does the opposite: the **doubt** is the biggest text on the screen and the answer is quiet under it.

That is the design, not a layout choice. A page that has spent eight blocks explaining itself has to show it can hear the objection before it asks for anything, and the way to show that is to let the objection be the loudest thing and not soften it on the way in. A guard measures it — every doubt's computed font size against its answer's — because an inversion that survives only in a docblock is not one.

## Every answer names something the product does

Not something it intends. The unscored area, the branch of its own, the machine holding no credential, the fast-forward or nothing, the free Product Scan, the day that says nothing needs you, the single setup step. Each is enforced somewhere in this repository and said elsewhere on this page; here they are answers rather than features, which is a different job for the same facts.

**The first one admits Vibe is wrong.** *"Sometimes it will."* It is the sentence a rewrite deletes first and the reason the other four are believable, so a guard pins it.

## Nobody is quoted

The five sentences are objections, not testimonials, and no name, face or attribution sits beside any of them — because nobody said them. A page that credited a doubt to a person who does not exist would be the fabricated record the truthfulness rules forbid, and it is the same rule that keeps invented metrics off this page. The guard asserts no `cite`, no image, and nothing shaped like an em-dash attribution.

Three guards, three mutations: the doubt shrunk below its answer, *"Sometimes it will"* softened to *"It rarely is"*, and a founder's name added after a quote. All caught.
